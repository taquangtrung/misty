# Changelog

All notable changes to misty are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).

While misty is at `0.0.x`, any release may change the public API; each such change is listed here under **Changed** or **Removed** with what to use instead. A major version bump of `wgpu` or `accesskit`, which misty re-exports, is a misty breaking change.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- `Theme::match_text` (`ThemeToken::MatchText`, `match-text` in a theme file): the color a picker's matched characters are drawn in, apart from `Theme::accent`, which the built-in themes keep it at and which a theme file setting `accent` alone carries it to. The quick pick's prompt stays in the accent.
- The settings page as elements of the page kit, each controlled: `view::settings_page` (sidebar, filter field with `SettingsPage::FILTER_ID`, scope tabs), `view::settings_section` and `view::setting_row` (the control a setting's kind takes, a scope note, and a Reset button in a later scope, edits heard as a `SettingChange`), and `view::keybindings_section` (each command's keys as key caps, a conflict in the danger color with a sentence naming the other command, changes heard as a `KeyChange`). `CommandRegistry::edit_keys` makes a `KeyEdit`; `Setting::with_value`, `SettingsSection::set_value`, `title`, and `settings` serve an app holding the values.
- Fields for richer values, as widgets and as elements: `ColorField` (`view::color_field`, a color typed as `#rrggbb` beside a swatch), `NamesField` (`view::names_field`, names typed as words), and `KeyCapture` (`view::key_capture`, a command's keys rebound by pressing the new one). A typed value applies once it reads as one, the user's draft kept while the field has focus. `Strings::settings_reset` (`settings-reset`) words the Reset button.
- `view::select_owned`: a dropdown of options built for the view rather than lent.
- `ViewHost::move_focus(delta)` moves the keyboard as Tab and Shift+Tab do, for an app's own keys; `ViewHost::ime_area()` says where the focused field's caret is, for an input method.
- `StatusSegment::progress()` marks where the notifier's progress item goes, on either side or after the primary text (`StatusLine::with_after_primary`); `with_details(false)` leaves the task's step out. `StatusSegment::PROGRESS_ID` names the item for a tooltip.
- `ScrollView::target()`: where a glide under way ends.

- `view::TabStrip::on_tab_drop(apply)`: a strip taking a `DraggedTab` dropped on it from another strip, its own marker showing the gap it would land in while the drag is over it, `apply` running with the tab's key and that place. `Element::drag_source_with(mods, payload, label)`: a drag source started only by a left press holding exactly `mods` (Alt to carry a document's view where a plain drag selects), that press going to none of the element's other pointer callbacks.
- Tabs dragged between strips and panes: a tab dragged off a `view::tab_strip` is a drag between elements carrying a `view::DraggedTab<K>` with its key, its title following the pointer, so an element whose `Element::drop_target` takes a `DraggedTab<K>` (another strip, a pane to split) is marked under it and gets it where it is dropped; back on its strip, or with Esc, the drag ends and the strip reorders as before. `Element::drop_marker(marker)` marks only the part of a drop target a drag is over (a pane's side, with `DropZone::at` and `DropZone::rect`) rather than all of it.
- Tab strip overflow buttons: when the tabs do not all fit, a previous-tab and a next-tab button (chevrons, `Icon::ChevronLeft` and `ChevronRight`) step through them beside the new-tab button, named for screen readers by the new `Strings::prev_tab` and `next_tab` (`prev-tab`, `next-tab`); `TabStrip::with_min_tab_width(width)` keeps every tab at least that wide.
- Tab strip looks: `TabStrip::with_tab_borders(bool)` draws a border down each side of every tab, on by default; `with_active_tab_radius(radius)` rounds the active tab's top corners, its accent following them; `set_window_active(bool)` dims the accent line while the window has lost the focus; `set_accent_overhang(pixels)` has the accent line reach over a border the app draws along the strip's top, and `accent_rect()` says where it was drawn.
- `AppCx::show_hints()`: hint labels, as Vimium puts on links, over everything of the window that a screen reader can click (a button, each tab of a strip, each title bar button, a pressable element) or that takes typed text, inside the innermost modal layer while one shows, on the next frame. Typing a label clicks its target as a screen reader does, and a text field gets the keyboard instead; Esc, a pointer press, or a wheel turn takes the labels away, and while they show they take every key, as `Hints` does. An app that never calls it sees no change.
- `NoticeLog::remove(index)`: forgets one notification by its place from the newest, as a log page's Delete does, handing it back.
- `view::breadcrumb(segments)`: where a view is as a row of crumbs, as an element over the `Breadcrumb` widget for a pane's or a page's header; `Breadcrumb::on_navigate(apply)` runs with the index of the segment the user goes to (a click, or Left, Right, and Enter while it has the keyboard, or the menu of the crumbs that do not fit). The widget now implements `Control`, taking its labels from `BreadcrumbProps`.
- The `files` feature: picker sources over the file system in `picker::files`. `FileBrowser::files` and `FileBrowser::folders` show one folder at a time (a `.` row for the folder shown, its folders named with a trailing `/`, then its files unless choosing a folder), `Tab` or `Enter` going into a folder, `Backspace` in an empty query going up, and a path typed and ended with `/` going there (`/` alone to the root, `~` to the home folder given with `with_home`); choosing hands the app's `choose` a `Chosen` (a file, the folder shown, or, with `with_new_file`, a file to make named as typed). The app sets the words (`BrowserWords`), the keys going up or choosing the folder shown from its keymap (`with_keys`, `BrowserKey`), how a typed path is read (`with_typed_paths`), where folders are read (`with_reader`, a `FolderReader`; `LocalFolders` by default, `with_extensions` and `with_hidden`), and how rows rank (`with_ranker`, a `Ranker`; the picker matches by words without one). `FileScan::new` lists every file of some extensions under some folders, found on a thread, by name with its path as a hint.
- Paragraph indents: `Paragraph::with_first_line_indent(indent)` starts the first line `indent` logical pixels in, as CSS's `text-indent` does, and `Paragraph::with_hanging_indent(indent)` the lines after it, so a bullet or a number hangs left of the text the wrapped lines line up with; measuring (`Frame::measure_paragraph`, `paragraph_width`, `ParagraphMeasurer`), drawing, rendering to an image, and the layout's lines, carets, selections, hit tests, links, and inline boxes all follow them. A paragraph with neither is shaped and drawn as before.
- `Element::chrome_labels()` on a `view::form`: its labels set as chrome, in the theme's `ChromeText` font as buttons and headers are (the proportional font in the built-in themes), measured with the frame so the label column is as wide as the widest label so set; without it a form's labels stay on the cell grid, so existing forms keep their layout.
- Masked text fields: `TextField::with_mask()` and `set_masked(masked)`, and `view::TextField::masked()`, draw a dot for each character of the text (on the cell grid or in the proportional font, the caret, selection, and pointer presses on the same characters), never copy or cut it to the clipboard nor write it to the primary selection (pasting still works), select it all on a double click rather than a word, show no input method composition, and describe it to screen readers as a `PasswordInput` holding only dots; `TextField::text` and `on_change` still hand the app the text.
- `view::Dialog::field(text, apply)` and `field_placeholder(placeholder)`: a dialog asking for a line of text, a text field under its body (the hand-driven `Dialog::with_content` room) that has the keyboard when the dialog opens and takes the input method, `apply` running with the new text as the user edits it; Tab and Shift+Tab move on to the buttons and back, Enter in the field chooses the first primary button, and Esc runs `on_close`.
- `view::Overlay::keys_to_app()`: a modal layer that hands the keys its elements leave to `App::key`, as the window does without one, so the app's own Esc closes it and its chords still work; Tab still cycles inside it, the elements under it still get no key or pointer, and a menu or popup opened over it keeps the keyboard as before. Without it a modal layer keeps such keys, as before.
- Spreadsheet cells in `Table`, each off until asked for, so a table that asks for none draws as before: `TableSource::cell_style(row, column)` returns a `TableCellStyle` (`with_fill(color)` filling the whole cell, padding and all, under its text; `with_borders(CellBorders)`, lines of a `CellBorder::new(color, width)` on any edge, `CellBorders::all(border)`, centered on the grid line so neighbours share one line; `with_align(align)` in place of the column's); `TableSource::merged_range(row, column)` returns the `CellRange` a cell is merged into, drawn once across its rows and columns with its top left cell's content and style, centered down the range; `TableSource::draw_over(frame, cells, theme)` draws over the cells (a chart anchored to a range) placed by `TableCells::cell_rect` and `TableCells::range_rect`; `Table::with_frozen(rows, columns)` and `set_frozen` keep leading rows and columns in place while the others scroll under them; `Table::with_zoom(zoom)` and `set_zoom` draw the cells' text, rows, and widths `zoom` times their size, widths saved and dragged staying unzoomed; `Table::set_cell_cursor(Some(column))` outlines a cell on the cursor's row in the accent color, moved by Left and Right (`h`, `l`), `0`, `$`, and a click (`TableResponse::CellCursorMoved(column)`), the columns scrolling sideways to keep it in sight. The `view::table` element takes them as `frozen(rows, columns)`, `zoom(zoom)`, `cell_cursor(column)`, and `on_cell_cursor_move(apply)`.
- Looks for tree buttons: `view::Button::variant(variant)` draws a `view::button` or `view::icon_button` in a `ButtonVariant`, as the hand-driven `Button::with_variant` does: `Ghost` is flat at rest and takes the theme's subtle hover fill only under the pointer and while pressed (a toggle pressed in takes the selection fill), with no shadow or lift and the hover fading as a color only, for a toolbar's buttons; `Primary` and `Danger` fill it with the accent or the danger color. The focus ring is drawn around it in every look. Buttons without it keep the default look.
- `Element::on_size_change(apply)`: the size an element was laid out at, as an `ElementSize` (width and height in physical pixels, the display's `scale_factor`, and `logical_width` and `logical_height` in the logical pixels of `Extent::Px`), reported once the frame that laid it out is drawn and only when it changed (and when first laid out), then drawn again, so the app has it for the next rebuild: a toolbar leaves out the buttons its group's width cannot hold without estimating that width itself. A window with no element changing size rebuilds nothing more; `TreeWindow::about_to_wait` reports sizes in tests.
- Icons on tree buttons: `view::Button::icon(svg)` shows a one-color `Svg` (as title bar app buttons take) left of a button's label, and `view::icon_button(svg, name)` makes a button showing only the icon, square, whose `name` is its tooltip and its name for screen readers, so no icon-only button goes unnamed (a `button("")` given an icon and no name logs a warning saying to use `icon_button`). The icon is drawn in the theme's text color, muted while disabled, even on a toggle pressed in, whose label takes the accent. The hand-driven `Button`, `Toolbar`, `Dialog`, and `Breadcrumb` faces keep their widths, heights, and drawing, which a test pins.
- Headings at the app's own sizes: `Heading::SCALES` (1.8, 1.5, 1.25, 1.1, 1.0, 0.9) and `Heading::level_scale(level)` are how much larger each level sets its spans than their own size, now public; `BlockStyle::with_heading_scale(scale)` on a `Block::Styled` sets the headings inside it at `scale` in place of the level's (`1.0` keeps the sizes the app gave their spans), the innermost style that sets one winning. The headings keep their level, role, anchors, and weight. Headings outside such a style are set as before.
- `Block::Styled(BlockStyle, blocks)`: a document's blocks set as a `BlockStyle` says, any block (a heading, a paragraph, a list, a table, code) taking its own line height with `BlockStyle::new().with_line_height(multiple)`, as `Paragraph::with_line_height` sets a paragraph's: the innermost style that sets one wins, and it wins over the view's `RichText::set_line_height`, so a reader sets its headings at 1.25 among body text at 1.7. Headings inside keep their role and anchors; a highlighted code block keeps the line height once colored. Adding the variant leaves `Block` matches compiling, as it is `#[non_exhaustive]`, and documents without it are laid out as before.
- `on_row_key(accepts, apply)` on `view::List`, `view::Table` (`apply` given the row index) and `view::Tree` (given the item's key): a key the element left unused on the cursor's row (`x` to mark a reading item read) goes to the app with that row while `accepts` says yes to it, as `Canvas::on_key` takes keys. The element's own keys (motions, counts, a prefix and the key completing or dropping it, Space, Enter) never reach `accepts`, so it cannot take them; a key it declines, or one with no row under the cursor (an empty list, a tree's loading row), goes on to `App::key` as before, and a count the key dropped is still reported through `on_keys`.
- `view::Table::proportional()` and `view::Tree::proportional()`: a table's cells or a tree's labels set in the proportional (UI) font, as `view::List::proportional` sets a list's rows, through the hand-driven widgets' `with_proportional_font`; the header and a tree's arrows and indents stay as they were. Fixed when the element is first built.
- `view::Custom::ime_caret(caret)`: an embedded hand-driven widget takes text from the input method while it has the keyboard, as misty's text fields do: `caret` says where its text caret was drawn (or `None` while it takes no text, as in a Vim Normal mode), the input method is enabled with its candidate window there, and the widget gets the composition as `Input::Ime` (preedit, then commit); without it, the input method stays disabled for the element as before. `TreeWindow::ime_area` gives where the input method is anchored, for tests sending `Input::Ime`.
- Pages in `RichText`: `RichText::set_paged(Some(Paged::new(columns)))` (`Paged::with_gap` for the room between pages) lays the text out a column wide and cuts it into pages as tall as the view, a spread of `columns` pages side by side, no line ever cut at a page's foot (an image or custom block that fits a page moves whole to the next), with no scrollbar. `page_count`, `page` (the left page of the spread shown), `page_of(pos)`, `page_start(page)`, `set_page`, and `turn_pages(spreads)` (which says whether the page changed, so a turn past the last spread can open the next chapter) read and turn them; Space, Page Down, Shift+Space, Page Up, Home, and End turn them while the view has the keyboard, and the wheel is left to the app. Selecting, marks, the app caret, find, `reveal`, anchors, custom blocks, and screen readers work on every page shown, the place read is kept when the view is resized or its line height changes, and switching between paged and scrolled keeps it too. `RichText::place_rect(pos)` gives where a place shows in the window, on any page or scrolled, or `None` out of sight. `make bench` times the chapter paged and turned a spread a frame.
- `Block::Aligned(align, blocks)`: a document's paragraphs and headings inside it (in lists, quotes, boxes, and table cells too) set their lines at the start, centered, at the end, or justified (`TextAlign`), as CSS's inherited `text-align`; code keeps to the start and the innermost alignment wins. Adding the variant leaves `Block` matches compiling, as it is `#[non_exhaustive]`.
- Line height: `Paragraph::with_line_height(multiple)` sets each line `multiple` times its font size tall, as CSS's unitless `line-height` (`1.7` for loose reading text), the extra room shared above and below the text and a larger span's line taller in step; measuring, `ParagraphMeasurer`, drawing, carets, selections, and hit tests all follow it. `RichText::set_line_height(Some(multiple))` sets it on all of a view's text (a document's headings, lists, tables, and code alike), keeping a paragraph's own, while the gaps between blocks stay as they were. Paragraphs without it are shaped and drawn as before.
- `RichText::set_scrollbar_hidden(true)` leaves a rich text's scrollbar out, the text laid out across the whole width, for columns side by side that would each show one; keys, the wheel, and `scroll_by` still scroll it.
- `RichText::scroll_by(ScrollAmount)` scrolls by `ScrollAmount::Pixels` or `ScrollAmount::Lines` (the line Up and Down scroll by), gliding as keys do, and says whether the view moved, so a reader turning past the top or the bottom of a chapter knows to open the next one; `RichText::content_height` is how tall the laid out text is, beside `scroll_position` and `set_scroll_position`.
- `make bench` times a book chapter in a `RichText` (30 headings over 300 wrapped paragraphs): laid out anew and drawn without the GPU, and scrolled a few pixels a frame.
- `App::key_released(key, cx)`, with a default that does nothing: a key let go that no element took, for what lasts while a key is held (a scroll speeding up), even while a modal layer keeps the keys; `App::key` still sees presses only, so tree apps that do not implement the hook see no new calls. No release comes for a key held as the window loses the keyboard. `TreeWindow::release(keys)` lets go of keys in tests. A hand-driven `Application` already gets releases as `Input::KeyReleased`, unchanged. winit reports releases on X11 (with detectable autorepeat, so a held key repeats without releases between) and Wayland alike.
- `Element::on_pointer_press(apply)`: a pointer press on an element, as `on_press` reports it, without making the element a tab stop that Enter, Space, and screen readers press, for content that already has its own keyboard path (pages driven by the app's keymap, a canvas with keys of its own).
- `view::Canvas::focusable()`: a canvas that takes the keyboard (a tab stop with a focus ring, focused when pressed, as a control and a canvas with `on_key` are) though it takes no keys itself, every key going on to the app, so the keyboard rests on content the app drives with its own keymap rather than on a button that would take Enter or Space.
- Tab order across containers: `Element::order` on a container (a column, a row, a form, a split, a scrolling container) visits the tab stops inside it as one block, the block taking that order among the elements beside it in the tree, so a toolbar drawn above a canvas can come after it while the rest of the window keeps its place. `order` on a container did nothing before; on a control it works as before.
- `view::TextField::on_submit(apply)` and `on_cancel(apply)`: what Enter and Esc (no modifiers) in a text field run on the app's state; the field then takes the key, so it reaches no other element nor `App::key`. Without them, Enter and Esc go on as before.
- `Element::on_pointer_move(apply)`: where the pointer is over an element, from its top left in physical pixels (`Some(point)` on each move, whatever is inside it, and while a press on it is held), and `None` once when it leaves, for following the mark or link under the pointer on a canvas. Each move already rebuilds the tree and redraws once, so the callback adds no rebuild.
- `view::Text::proportional`: a plain text element set in the proportional (UI) font rather than on the cell grid, as `view::List::proportional` and the widgets' `with_proportional_font` set theirs: a row gives it the width its shaped glyphs take, it stays a line tall, and it is cut with an ellipsis when its room is short.
- Keyboard reach for `view::tab_strip`: a strip with tabs is a tab stop, so Tab and `AppCx::focus` land on it; Left and Right move between its tabs, wrapping, the one the keyboard is on outlined while it is not the active tab, and Enter or Space activates it as a click does.
- `Toolbar::natural_width`: how wide a toolbar's items are with none hidden (buttons and groups at their faces' widths, separators, and the gaps between items, spacers taking nothing), in physical pixels, so an app can size a toolbar to its buttons and lay another widget beside it; drawn that wide it shows every button and no "more" button.
- A test clock for tree apps: `TreeWindow::advance(by)` holds the clock misty reads on the test's thread and moves it on without sleeping, then steps the tree's animations, delays (tooltips, which-key popups, submenus, caret blinks, glides, toasts) and the app's timers at the new time; `TreeWindow::now` reads it, and inputs sent after count presses in a row by it. The clock follows the system's again when the window is dropped, and nothing changes for a test that never advances it. The quick pick's query pause does not follow it yet.
- `PhysicalKey` and `EventCx::physical_key`: where the key being handled sits, named by what a US layout types there without Shift, with every modifier held, Shift included, so Ctrl+Shift+/ arrives as the key `C-?` and the physical key `/` with Ctrl and Shift on any layout. A tree app reads it in `App::key` through `cx.event_cx()`; `TestWindow::set_physical_key` sets it in tests. `Key`, `Key::new`, key equality, and the key notation are unchanged (checked against how junction-rs, winter-term, and polar-dict build and compare keys).
- `WindowOptions::with_active(false)` opens a window without taking the keyboard or coming to the front, for test and benchmark runs: through the system's request on Windows and macOS, and on X11 by marking the window, before it first shows, with the EWMH hint that it wants no focus (`_NET_WM_USER_TIME` 0), which EWMH window managers honor. Wayland lets no client ask; misty passes no activation token there, so the compositor decides. misty now names `x11rb` (already built by winit) as a direct dependency on Linux and the BSDs for it.
- `AppCx::focus(id)`: a tree app moves the keyboard and the focus ring to the element with that `Element::id` when the window is next drawn, for spatial pane moves: a control takes it, a container passes it to its first tab stop unless the keyboard is inside it already, and an open trap keeps it out. An id no element has, or an element with nothing to focus, is logged as a warning saying how to fix it. Callbacks ask through the app's state and `App::about_to_wait`, which has an `AppCx`.
- `Theme::emphasis`, an `Emphasis` (`Bold`, the default, or `Regular`): how misty's chrome stresses words (status toasts and prompts, the mode badge, which-key headers, keycaps, transient and dialog titles and headings, hint labels, menu headings, and the settings page's titles, key prompt, and modified values), so an app drawn at one weight sets `Emphasis::Regular`. Theme files take it as an optional top-level `emphasis "bold"` or `emphasis "regular"` (`ThemeFileError::UnknownEmphasis` otherwise); files without it, and how `shape` is read, are unchanged. The quick pick's match highlights stay bold for now, and `fuzzy_highlight` still bolds its matches.
- Menu headings: `MenuEntry::Heading(label)` names the items under it, drawn muted, so a hamburger menu shows File, Edit, View, and Help as sections rather than submenus; Up and Down, the pointer, and a click pass over it, it is never chosen, and screen readers read it as a heading. `Menu` has no type-ahead, so none skips it.
- App buttons in the title bar: `view::TitleBar::app_button(button, apply)` adds a `TitleBarButton` (`TitleBarButton::icon(svg, tooltip)` or `TitleBarButton::label(text)`, with `with_icon` and `with_tooltip`) at the end of the bar's room beside the menu button (beside the window buttons when those sit at the right). It runs `apply` when clicked, clicked by a screen reader, or pressed with Enter or Space; the bar's buttons are one tab stop that Left and Right move through, and each button's tooltip shows where the pointer rests. The hand-driven `TitleBar` takes them with `with_app_buttons` and `set_app_buttons`, answers `TitleBarResponse::AppButton(index)` (hit `TitleBarHit::AppButton(index)`), and gives each one's place and help through `app_button` and `tooltip_hover`.
- `TreeItem::section`: a row that titles the items after it, as a sidebar's "Bookmarks" does, drawn small in the muted color with no arrow and described to screen readers as a heading. The cursor's motions (counts too) step over it, a click on it does nothing, and it is never selected, by a Shift range or a rubber band alike, nor activated; a reload that leaves the cursor on one moves it to the next item, or the one before when none follows. Trees with no sections behave as before.
- Lent data elements in the element tree ([`docs/element-tree.md`](https://github.com/taquangtrung/misty/blob/main/docs/element-tree.md), phase 4): `view::list`, `view::table`, `view::tree_view`, and `view::icon_grid` show the app's rows lent for one rebuild, never copied, each taking the height its column leaves. Their cursor and selection come from the app's state and change only through `on_cursor_move` and `on_select`; the scroll, the keys typed toward a motion (reported by `on_keys`), drags, rubber bands, a tree's expanded nodes, and a table's dragged column widths stay with misty. Each reports what its widget answers through callbacks named after it: `on_activate`, `on_drop`, `on_sort`, `on_resize`, `on_header_menu`, `on_expand`, `on_collapse`, and `on_load`. `List::which_key` shows the which-key popup of a prefix left waiting.
- `view::cells` draws the app's `CellSource` on the cell grid, its cursor blinking at the theme's caret rate and starting over when it moves (`Cells::steady` holds it still).
- `view::quick_pick`, a type-to-filter picker as a modal layer: misty keeps its session (the query, the matches, the selection) while the layer shows, and reports `on_run` and `on_close`.
- `App::start` hands a tree app the `Waker` other threads send it `Update`s through, closures misty runs on the app's state before redrawing every window; `TreeWindow::waker` and `TreeWindow::deliver` do the same headlessly.
- `App::on_panic`, told when a callback, a key hook, or an update panicked and misty carried on, and `App::reduced_motion`, the app's own less-motion override.
- `TreeItems`, the items of a tree an app keeps and loads itself (roots, children, failures, `reload`), versioned so a tree copies them only after they change; `TableColumn::with_hidden` hides a column the app keeps.
- `make bench` times the list and a table of 100,000 rows as tree elements beside the same views drawn by hand.
- `EventCx::click_count`: how many presses of one button in a row the pointer press being handled makes (1 single, 2 double, 3 triple), counted by the runtime within the double-click time and a movement slop scaled to the display, and by `TestWindow` for presses sent in a row. A release reports its press's count. `ButtonInput` is unchanged, so inputs built as struct literals keep compiling.
- `FlowList::scroll_position` and `FlowList::set_scroll_position`: the row at the top and the pixels of it scrolled away, to save a reader's place and restore it exactly, even before the first frame.
- `FlowList::with_row_pointer`: presses, drags, and releases of the left button inside rows come back as `FlowListResponse::RowPointer` (a `RowPointer` with the row, a point in it, the `RowPointerKind`, the click count, and the modifiers), for selecting text inside rows, instead of moving the cursor and selecting rows; keys still move the cursor and the selection.
- `ParagraphLayout::lines`: each wrapped line of a paragraph as a `ParagraphLine` (the byte range of the text it holds, the ranges following one another without gaps, and its top, height, and baseline in the window), from `Frame::paragraph_layout`, `Frame::draw_paragraph`, and a `ParagraphMeasurer` off the UI thread alike, to split text into pages at line boundaries.
- `Jobs`: a small pool of worker threads that runs closures off the UI thread and hands each result to `Application::message` through a `Waker`, with no async runtime. `Jobs::spawn` returns a `JobHandle` that cancels the job when dropped: a queued job never starts and a running one's result is thrown away, so a stale result never reaches the app; `JobHandle::detach` lets a job finish regardless.
- `Deadlines`: a keyed set of moments things are due (`set`, which puts a key off, `clear`, `get`, `next` for `Application::about_to_wait`, and `take_due`), for debounce and settle timers.
- `Span::underline_color` and the `Span::underline_color` field: an underline (a link's too) in a theme or exact color of its own rather than the glyphs', drawn by paragraphs, labels, and styled text on the cell grid alike.
- `WindowOptions::with_texture_budget`: the most bytes of picture textures a window keeps on the GPU, for an app that pages through large pictures; the default stays 256 MiB.

Its API is unchanged; a few highlight names are finer (`keyword.control` where it gave `keyword`), which a theme lacking them colors by their first parts.

### Fixed

- `ViewHost::focus(name)` gives the keyboard to the named element of a hosted tree; it found none, the name being looked up outside the host's scope.

- `draw_keycap` draws a key as a keycap, a bold label on a chip a shade off the raised color, for a hints line or a dialog that names its keys.
- `QuickPick::with_window_share`: a picker placed in the window takes a share of its width and height, in the middle of it, as a desktop app's command palette does, rather than the size its rows need near the top.
- `Popup::header_strip` gives where a header strip goes across a popup, from one side of the box to the other just inside its border; `draw_popup_header` rounds the strip's top corners as the box's are. `QuickPick`, transient menus, and a centered `WhichKey` head themselves with it.
- `Popup::with_rect`, a popup filling a rect its owner placed, is public, for an app that lays out a box of its own and draws it as misty draws popups.
- `StatusBar::with_background` fills the bar with a theme color of the app's choosing, such as `ThemeToken::Background` for a bar that reads as part of the content above it; the chrome's surface color stays the default.
- `Frame::draw_window_outline` draws a hairline around a frameless window, a logical pixel thick, in the overlay; a maximized window gets none. Call it last, so nothing drawn after covers it.
- `WindowOptions::with_rounded_corners` rounds a frameless window's corners as modern desktops round windows: by the system on macOS (the window keeps a native frame whose title bar is hidden under the app's own, so the corners and the shadow are the system's) and on Windows 11, and by misty elsewhere, which makes the window transparent and cuts the corners out of every frame, antialiased; `Frame::corner_radius` says the radius in force, which `Frame::draw_window_outline` follows. A maximized window keeps square corners.

### Changed

- Each layer of a frame is painted in the order it was drawn: a box, path, picture, or text drawn later paints over anything drawn before it. Before, every layer painted its fills, then its pictures, then its text, then its decorations, whatever the order of the calls, so a highlight filled after `Frame::draw_image` or `Frame::draw_texture` was hidden under the picture. Draw a background before what sits on it. Consecutive draws of one kind are still painted together, so drawing all backgrounds and then all labels costs fewer draw calls than alternating them.
- `Frame::fill_rect_over_text` now paints like `Frame::fill_rect`, in call order; draw it after the text it marks.
- A picker ranks the rows of one level (whole, from the start, in order, out of order) by how near the end of its field each row's match ends, so a file name at the end of a path comes before a directory early in another; a word found twice in a row is lit at its place nearest the end. `Query::score_row` and `Query::score_text` scores spread over the whole `u8` range accordingly.
- `SplitView`'s lent `Widget::draw` no longer draws the focused pane's outline, which the panes' content drawn after it would cover; call `SplitView::draw_focus_outline` after the content instead.
- The `height(metrics, scale_factor)` of `Button`, `Checkbox`, `RadioGroup`, `Select`, `Slider`, `Stepper`, and `TextField` is replaced by `Control::measure(width, metrics, scale_factor)`, which returns the same heights; import `misty::Control` and pass the width the control is laid out at (`f32::INFINITY` when it has none). `ProgressBar` and `TextArea` measure the same way: a bar a row, an area the rows its text wraps into.
- `RadioGroupProps`, `TextFieldProps`, and `TextAreaProps` have a new field, `label: Option<&str>`, the name screen readers give the control; a struct literal sets it, `None` for none.
- `TextAlign` has a new variant, `Justify`; a `match` over it needs an arm for it. Tables and rows set it at the start.
- `KeyScope::TextFocused` now leaves out `M-b`, `M-d`, `M-f`, and `M-y`, the Alt chords a focused text field edits with, so `CommandRegistry::bindings` and `CommandRegistry::conflicts` in that scope no longer offer them to the app. A behavior fix following the kill ring's `M-y`: a command bound to one of them still runs from a key the app dispatches while no field has focus.
- The built-in themes set their chrome (the title bar, tabs, the status bar, menus) in the proportional font and their boxes in `Shape::MODERN`, rounded with a soft shadow, as most desktop apps look; `ChromeText::default()` is now `Proportional`. Call `Theme::with_grid_look` for the monospace, square look they had.
- `Shape` has a new field, `row_padding`; a `Shape` written out field by field needs it.
- Window buttons at the left edge now read close, minimize, maximize, as macOS and GNOME's left layout place them, where they used to read close, maximize, minimize.
- A title bar centers its title by default on Linux and macOS, as those systems do; it stays at the start on Windows. `TitleBar::with_title_align(TitleAlign::Start)` keeps it at the start everywhere.
- `QuickPick` sets its rows (label, description, hint, detail) in the theme's chrome font, as its heading already was; the query field and the preview panel keep the cell grid.
- `QuickPick`'s heading is a popup header strip's height (`Popup::header_height`), its count centered on it, where it was a line.
- A transient menu sets its headings and its rows' labels in the theme's chrome font; keys stay on the cell grid.
- A `QuickPick` query drawn as a box (`QueryStyle::Field`) is in the proportional font, its placeholder with it, where the theme's chrome is; a prompted query keeps the cell grid.
- A `QuickPick`'s rows keep their icon and hint in from the band's sides past its highlight, its icons are 0.85 of a line (they were 0.75), and its hints line has the shape's row padding above and below it.
- Proportional text (chrome labels, paragraphs) is set in the desktop's interface font, the family GNOME's interface setting names (else the one fontconfig matches for `sans-serif`) on Linux, SF Pro on macOS, and Segoe UI on Windows, where one is installed; it was whatever family the font library took for sans-serif.
- A window draws text at the desktop's interface size (`Font::desktop`) unless `WindowOptions::with_font` says otherwise, where it was 15 logical pixels everywhere: 13.3 for GNOME's default of 10 points. `Font::default()` stays 15, as offscreen frames and tests draw in it.
- A `List`'s rows take the shape's row padding above and below their text, where they were a line tall; `Shape::GRID` keeps them so. Lists, tree views, and tables draw their highlight in `Shape::highlight_band`, and where it is inset the cursor's accent bar is a pill as tall as the band in the inset beside it rather than a bar along the row's edge.
- A `QuickPick` row leaves a gap between its icon and its label.
- A transient menu heads itself with `draw_popup_header`, as dialogs and pickers do.
- A paragraph sets the icon glyphs of a run that names no family (Unicode's Private Use Areas, where Nerd Fonts and Font Awesome keep them) in the window's cell font, so chrome labels in the proportional font show an app's icons as its grid does instead of whatever font falls back.
- Completion lists and transient menus draw their highlight as a `Shape::highlight` box, rounded like the other lists', where they filled a square band.
- A menu draws a submenu's `›` arrow muted, as it does accelerators, where it took the label's color.
- A menu's separators take the shape's row padding above and below, as its items do.
- The rows of a `QuickPick` and a transient menu take the shape's row padding above and below their text, and a completion list's rows half of it; `Shape::GRID` keeps them a line tall. A transient menu's title takes a header strip's height.

### Fixed

- A `QuickPick` showing more or fewer lines than twelve moved and paged its selection as if it showed twelve, so each key made the session fetch its rows again twice and the list flash while it scrolled; it now moves by the lines it drew.
- A `QuickPick`'s boxed query (`QueryStyle::Field`) sits centered between the heading and the line under it, with even room above and below, rather than on the line under it, and is as wide as a row's highlight rather than a column narrower on each side.
- A `QuickPick` whose box is no whole number of columns wide kept the remainder, and a column more, on the right of its rows and query field; its rows now sit evenly in the box, and its hints line at the box's foot, the room whole rows leave over above it rather than under it.
- A label set to end at its clip's edge, such as a picker's right-aligned count, no longer loses its last character (`1/` for `1/3`) when the subtraction placing it rounds to a hair less room than its width.
- A `ScrollView`'s resting thumb is drawn in `Theme::scrollbar`, the color a theme gives scrollbars, which no widget used; it was `Theme::border`.
- `Popup`, and so menus, context menus, selects, dialogs, the settings page, and `FloatingPanel`, fill with `Theme::raised`, the color a theme gives what floats over the content, rather than `Theme::surface`, the chrome's; they had stood apart from `QuickPick`, toasts, and completion lists, which already used `raised`.
- A picker (`QuickPick`, `command_palette`) accepting right after typing, before the app's next `about_to_wait`, ran the best match of the query before the last keys rather than of the query shown. Accept now takes in the matcher's answer to the query typed first, waiting at most a quarter of a second for a matcher on its worker thread.
